Single-Pass TEMA AEL Shell-and-Tube Heat Exchanger

Thermal Equipment Design · CAD + Fabrication
View on GrabCAD →
Software: Fusion 360 Config: TEMA AEL, Single-Pass Shell Ø: 152.4 mm Tube Length: 762 mm Layout: 30° Triangular Pitch
CAD + Physical Prototype Fabricated

A TEMA AEL-configuration shell-and-tube heat exchanger — removable channel and cover front head (A), single-pass shell (E), fixed tubesheet rear head (L) — modeled in Fusion 360 and carried through to a welded steel prototype. The assembly includes the shell, tube bundle, tube sheets, channel heads, nozzles, tie rods, baffles, and flanged bolted connections.

The 30° triangular tube layout was chosen over a square pitch for its higher heat transfer coefficient and tube density per shell diameter, accepting the trade-off of harder mechanical tube cleaning — a reasonable choice for a clean, non-fouling service.

From Design to Hardware

  • CAD assembly built part-by-part in Fusion 360, matching real TEMA nomenclature and fabrication tolerances
  • Tube sheet drilled and tubes fixed on the triangular pitch pattern, then welded into the shell
  • Flanged, bolted end connections allow the channel covers to be removed for tube-side cleaning without disturbing the shell
  • Fabricated in steel with a welded support stand for bench testing

Human-Following Smart Cart

Electromechanical System Design · Group Project, MEE 22
Team: 3 members Tracking: BLE RSSI + IMU Fusion Billing: RFID Controller: Raspberry Pi + Arduino + ESP32 BOM: BDT 15,948
Designed, Fabricated & Tested

A three-person team project (Group 02, MEE 22) addressing two problems at once: manually pushing a cart is inconvenient and inaccessible, and in-store checkout queues are slow because billing only happens at the register. The goal was a cart that follows its user autonomously indoors while tallying the total price of items placed inside it.

Design Process

Three concepts were generated and scored on a weighted decision matrix (accuracy 0.4, cost 0.3, reliability 0.2, simplicity 0.1):

  • Concept A — line-follower robot with barcode scanning (score 7.4)
  • Concept B — BLE RSSI + IMU sensor fusion with RFID billing (score 8.6 — selected)
  • Concept C — depth camera + LiDAR with weight-based billing (score 6.9)

Concept B was selected for the best balance of accuracy, reliability, and cost. A smartphone's IMU and Bluetooth RSSI are fused to estimate user heading and distance; a Raspberry Pi handles motion planning and sends PWM commands to an Arduino-driven motor stage, while an RFID scanner and ESP32 read product tags and display a running total on an LCD.

Specifications Met

  • Tracking accuracy ≤ 0.5 m, heading error ≤ 10°
  • Follow speed 0.3–0.5 m/s, 3–5 m tracking range
  • Runtime ≥ 2 hours, collision force < 50 N (ISO cobot limit)
  • Total build cost: BDT 15,948 against a BDT 15,000 target

RosRider

Autonomous Mobile Robot Platform · CAD
View on GrabCAD →
Layout: 3-Tier Circular Chassis Sensing: 360° LiDAR Compute: Raspberry Pi Drive: Differential, 2-Wheel + Caster Use case: SLAM / Nav2
CAD Design

A compact, TurtleBot-inspired mobile robot platform designed for ROS2 research and development. The three-tier circular chassis separates concerns cleanly: a top-mounted 360° LiDAR for perception, a middle tier carrying the Raspberry Pi compute unit and networking, and a bottom tier housing the differential drive motors, motor driver, and power distribution.

Designed as a platform for SLAM, autonomous navigation, and obstacle avoidance work — the layered architecture leaves clear space for additional sensors and embedded electronics without redesigning the base chassis.

Volvo EC650 Excavator

Full Assembly CAD · Complex Linkage Design
View on GrabCAD →
Components: 100+ Rotation: 360° Turret Actuation: Hydraulic Cylinders Propulsion: Chain-Driven Undercarriage
CAD Design

A full assembly model of a Volvo EC650 excavator with over 100 components, built to understand the kinematics of heavy earthmoving machinery rather than just its outward form.

Major Sub-assemblies

  • Undercarriage frame — base structure carrying the chain-driven track propulsion (sprocket, idler, shoes, rollers)
  • Turret bed — mounted on the undercarriage via a slew interface, allowing the entire upper structure to rotate 360° independent of track orientation
  • Cabin — operator compartment fixed to the turret bed
  • Boom → Dipper → Bucket — a pin-jointed serial-link excavation arm, each stage actuated by an independent hydraulic cylinder, with a 4-bar linkage driving bucket curl

The boom-dipper-bucket chain and the turret's rotational interface are the two mechanisms that make an excavator work — modeling them meant getting the pin joints, linkage geometry, and cylinder mounting points right, not just the exterior panels.
